Introduction

Agricultural waste as an energy resource

Indonesia produces over 150 million tonnes of agricultural waste each year, a large share of it coconut shell. Left unmanaged, this waste is typically burned in the open or left to decompose in landfills, both of which release greenhouse gases without recovering any of the material's value.

This project investigates whether coconut shell waste can be converted into biochar and used as the electrode material in a supercapacitor — an electrical component capable of storing and releasing electricity.

Life cycles

The device stores 4.59 Wh of energy per kilogram and can release 371.89 watts per kilogram. With a capacitance of 500.92 farads and a stable output of 1.35 V, the cell can provide backup power or short-term storage for a small solar-powered load. A charge-discharge cycle rating above 100,000 cycles suggests the material can withstand repeated fast charging without significant degradation, though this was not tested to failure within the scope of this project.

Conclusion

Biochar produced from coconut shell waste can be used as an electrode material in a supercapacitor. The material shows sufficient electric conductivity and capacity, with efficient distribution of stored electricity. These findings suggest coconut-shell biochar has potential as a low-cost, waste-derived electrode material — with the added benefit of diverting agricultural waste from open burning and landfill decomposition.

Future Development

Scaling BioCharge Technology

To commercialize the BIOCHARGE technology, future development should focus on scaling the coconut-shell EDLC into a modular consumer power bank that replaces traditional lithium cells with ultra-fast charging and eco-friendly materials.

By integrating biodegradable gel electrolytes and stackable pouch designs, the device can safely deliver high-surge energy for everyday electronics and emergency power needs while entirely bypassing lithium and cobalt supply chains.
